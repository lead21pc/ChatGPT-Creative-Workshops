## 0. ROLE

This file defines the aesthetic base for request analysis, prompt composition, and result verification. It does not independently define subject identity, content, exact counts, anatomy, topology, attachment, props, setting, operation-specific composition, reference roles, or an operation stop condition.

Here, **sexy** does not default to exposed skin, sexualized posing, exaggerated breasts or hips, or skin-tight clothing. It is an aesthetic effect produced when **body, clothing, layering, line, color, and material lock together as one intentional visual structure with a coherent language**.

The current explicit request overrides only the fields it explicitly changes or releases. Every compatible field remains binding. Never add an attribute merely because it commonly accompanies a named keyword, genre, period, culture, occupation, or style.

### 0.1. Authority and limits

- `config.md` is an aesthetic base, not a complete operation lock and not a substitute for the current request.
- `SUBJECT_IDENTITY`, `EXACT_COUNTS`, `ANATOMY_TOPOLOGY`, `ATTACHMENT_RULES`, `REFERENCE_ASSIGNMENTS`, and `OPERATION_STOP_CONDITION` are `UNSET_BY_THIS_FILE`.
- `UNSET_BY_THIS_FILE` requires resolution by the current request or another router-authorized source. It never authorizes model invention.
- Activate a criterion only when the subject contains the corresponding component. Do not force body–clothing rules onto landscapes, architecture, objects, symbols, or subjects without those components.
- No aesthetic success may compensate for an error in identity, count, anatomy, topology, attachment, coverage, required visibility, or a prohibition.
- Never expand a local edit into a redesign.

### 0.2. Primary domain

- Prioritize human and humanoid character design, especially relationships among body, hair, clothing, footwear, accessories, and pose.
- Apply relevant rules to other subjects only when the corresponding components exist. Never impose human structure on a nonhuman subject.
- A style, period, culture, occupation, or genre label does not authorize a conventional costume bundle, accessory set, palette, status, narrative, or motif.

## 1. BASE ATOMIC COMPONENTS

### P01 — Overall silhouette

- Read the character first as a large, legible mass.
- The silhouette must have discernible rhythm, balance, and direction.
- Clothing layers must construct the silhouette rather than merely cover the body.
- Avoid fragmented contours, arbitrary volume, and competing focal points.

### P02 — Proportional system

- Relationships among head, shoulders, waist, hips, limbs, and clothing mass must be intentional.
- Stylization is allowed, but all proportions must belong to one design system.
- Do not default to contemporary realistic human proportions when an authorized style requires another system.

### P03 — Body curves

- Treat curves as visual paths, not as body parts to enlarge.
- Prefer continuous transitions across shoulder–waist–hip–leg or equivalent design paths.
- Never substitute anatomical exaggeration for structure.

### P04 — Body–clothing integration

- Clothing must receive, extend, counterbalance, or intentionally interrupt body lines.
- Every important seam, edge, belt, fold, or opening must relate to the form beneath it.
- Avoid a body and clothing that read as two unrelated image layers.

### P05 — Clothing layers

- Every layer must build mass, create depth, direct the eye, balance proportion, or establish rhythm.
- Inner, middle, and outer layers must remain legible.
- Overlaps must be clean and structurally coherent. Never add a layer solely to increase complexity.

### P06 — Intentional coverage

- Treat coverage as an active design tool.
- A highly covered silhouette can remain attractive through precise form, proportion, layering, and material behavior.
- Any opening or exposed area must serve rhythm or focus. Do not default to increasing exposed skin.

### P07 — Line language

- Preserve any authorized dominant line family: soft, taut, sharp, heavy, flowing, geometric, organic, or a specified combination.
- If no line family is established, do not invent one merely to complete the design.
- Outer contour, seams, pattern, hair, accessories, and pose must speak the same line language.
- Use line contrast only for a defined function.

### P08 — Visual rhythm

- Distribute curves, straight segments, rests, folds, and details with controlled rhythm.
- Preserve quiet regions so complex regions can function.
- Avoid uniform detail density.

### P09 — Focal hierarchy

- Establish primary focus, secondary focus, and rest areas only when composition is open to change.
- In edits or reconstructions, preserve the existing hierarchy unless explicitly released.
- Contrast, brightness, fine detail, and accessories must not all compete for primary focus.
- The face need not be the strongest focus when the authorized design centers silhouette or clothing.

### P10 — Color harmony

- When several color roles exist, define dominant, supporting, and accent roles. Do not require all three in monochrome, two-color, or restricted palettes.
- Color must reinforce layering, hierarchy, and the active visual language.
- Do not add colors merely to make the image lively.
- Under monochrome or no-added-color constraints, preserve that limit and add no accent color.

### P11 — Value structure

- Light, middle, and dark masses must clarify silhouette and layer order.
- Different materials must not collapse into one unreadable mass.
- Reserve high contrast for authorized focal areas.

### P12 — Material language

- Each material must have appropriate drape, tension, thickness, reflectivity, and fold behavior.
- Materials must complement or intentionally contrast one another.
- Do not give every fabric identical physical behavior and distinguish them only by surface pattern.

### P13 — Structure and drape

- Rigid garments must hold form; soft garments must respond coherently to gravity and the body.
- Folds must originate from suspension, tension, compression, or motion.
- Do not add source-less decorative folds that obscure structure.

### P14 — Detail density

- Concentrate detail by region and preserve hierarchy.
- Fine detail must not destroy the large silhouette.
- Remove details that do not contribute to the active design language.

### P15 — Accessories as structural components

- Accessories must extend line, balance mass, create rhythm, or lock a focal point.
- Their scale, placement, and material must belong to the whole system.
- Never attach accessories as isolated decoration or gap filler.

### P16 — Pose and body force

- Pose must communicate weight, body axis, and force direction.
- It must support the silhouette and the way clothing falls.
- Avoid default sexualized posing, implausible twisting, or poses used only to display separate body parts.

### P17 — Style consistency

- Proportion, line, clothing, material, palette, and stylization level must share one style logic.
- Do not mix aesthetic conventions without a defined primary–secondary relationship.
- A style reference supplies only its assigned attributes. It does not import its entire convention bundle.

### P18 — Distinctive identity

- Preserve an authorized signature through silhouette, layering, line paths, color relationships, or material structure.
- Develop a new signature only when the operation authorizes new design.
- A signature must emerge from relationships among parts, not from an unusual ornament attached afterward.
- Do not add identity markers merely to make the result feel less anonymous.

### P19 — Target-relative anatomy

- Anatomy must be correct for the authorized morphological system, not automatically for a contemporary human body.
- For cartoon, creature, stylized, or nonhuman forms, preserve specified limb and digit counts, construction, roundness, length, and deformation rules.
- Do not normalize an intentional nonstandard feature into an ordinary human body.

### P20 — Whole-form relationship

- All atomic components must converge into one coherent whole.
- A locally attractive component that damages the whole may be changed or removed only when it lies within the allowed variables. Otherwise report the conflict without expanding scope.
- Judge the result by relationships among parts, not by the number of individually satisfied checklist items.

### P21 — Head–torso–foot linkage

- Hair, headwear, neckline, and shoulder line must form an intentional transition.
- Hem, skirt, or trouser structure must relate to footwear, feet, and ground contact.
- Do not let head, torso, and feet operate as unrelated design languages.
- Footwear and headwear are not default containers for extra detail.
- Use accessories at the top and bottom to balance mass only when that relationship is authorized; never add them merely for decorative symmetry.

## 2. COMPOSITION RULES

1. **Large form before detail:** lock silhouette, proportion, force axis, and layering before pattern, accessories, or micro-detail.
2. **Relationships before isolated attributes:** evaluate how parts strengthen one another, not merely whether each part is attractive.
3. **One dominant language:** every choice must trace back to one design logic. Any exception requires an explicit counterbalancing function.
4. **Coverage does not reduce attraction:** do not increase exposed skin to compensate for weak silhouette, layering, or materials.
5. **No trope in place of structure:** do not use an extremely small waist, excessive leg length, enlarged breasts or hips, parted lips, an arched back, or seductive posing as shortcuts.
6. **No detail in place of design:** high detail without hierarchy or a shared language fails.
7. **Respect counts and morphology:** verify digits, limbs, clothing layers, accessories, colors, and negative constraints independently.
8. **Do not silently reconcile conflicts:** expose material conflicts instead of dropping one side.
9. **No compensation for hard failure:** whole-image polish cannot offset errors in identity, count, anatomy, topology, attachment, coverage, or prohibition.

### 2.1. Garment style grammar

Resolve an authorized style into relevant controllable properties:

- silhouette system;
- proportions and waist placement;
- shoulder construction;
- length and opening of bodice, sleeves, skirt, or trousers;
- layering logic;
- closure, fastening, suspension, or tying logic;
- material stiffness, softness, thickness, and drape;
- location and density of ornament;
- relationships among hair, clothing, footwear, and accessories;
- degree of stylization.

A style name is only an entry point into visual language. It does not authorize a full conventional bundle. When several influences are authorized:

- establish one primary system;
- assign exact attributes to each secondary influence;
- define their points of contact;
- do not import complete surface-symbol bundles;
- do not use decoration to hide structural incompatibility.

### 2.2. Garment-component roles

Every component must serve at least one established role:

- `PRIMARY_FORM`: build the main mass or silhouette;
- `SECONDARY_FORM`: connect, balance, or counterweight the main form;
- `TRANSITION`: resolve a transition between regions or layers;
- `FUNCTIONAL_CLOSURE`: close, tie, suspend, hold, or protect;
- `FOCAL_ACCENT`: create one controlled accent;
- `RHYTHMIC_REPEAT`: repeat in a limited way to reinforce rhythm;
- `IDENTITY_MARKER`: preserve an authorized identifying trait;
- `MATERIAL_CONTRAST`: clarify a material relationship.

Remove any component without an established role before execution. Do not retain an item merely because it is attractive, genre-compatible, or makes the design appear more detailed.

### 2.3. Accessory classes and budget

Classify accessories by function:

1. `STRUCTURAL`: hold, connect, suspend, tighten, protect, or change garment behavior.
2. `SEMANTIC`: communicate identity, role, culture, or status already established by an authorized source.
3. `FOCAL`: create an authorized visual accent.
4. `AMBIENT_DECORATION`: only increase density or generic atmosphere.

Prefer `STRUCTURAL`, then justified `SEMANTIC` or `FOCAL` accessories. Do not add `AMBIENT_DECORATION` by default. An item may serve several roles, but never invent a function, history, or meaning to justify an unauthorized item.

When quantity is unresolved, prefer one primary accessory cluster and at most one secondary cluster when required to balance form or connect the visual language. This is not a hard cap when an authorized source specifies more, but every cluster must have a distinct function and must not compete for the same role.

Do not add accessories to fill empty space, simulate luxury or complexity, conceal weak garment structure, distribute detail evenly, repeat a motif without adding a relationship, or make the image feel conventionally complete.

### 2.4. Detail-removal gate

Before including a layer, accessory, pattern, or accent in the tool instruction, ask:

1. Which established visual relationship does it reinforce?
2. Would removing it weaken identity, structure, hierarchy, or rhythm?
3. Is another component already performing its role more effectively?
4. Would it create a competing focal point?
5. Does it come from an authorized request or established design logic, or only from genre convention?

If question 1 has no answer, remove it. If removal weakens no authorized function and the component is not explicitly required, remove it. Never use this gate to delete an explicitly required component.

## 4. OPERATION-LOCK INTERFACE

After the router loads and resolves all required sources, construct an internal operation lock containing:

- **Operation:** `ANALYZE`, `CREATE`, `EDIT`, `VARIATION`, or `CORRECT`, exactly as requested.
- **Invariants:** identity, counts, anatomy, topology, silhouette, coverage, composition, palette, visual language, protected regions, and every property that must remain unchanged.
- **Allowed variables:** only properties explicitly opened by the request or an authorized source.
- **Forbidden transformations:** active prohibitions, relevant known failures, unauthorized additions, and spillover changes.
- **Unresolved fields:** every field lacking an authorized value; handle only under `IMAGE_ANTI_PRIOR.md`.
- **Stop condition:** the exact point at which the request is complete. After it is reached, do not decorate, beautify, modernize, narrativize, or extend the result.

Only after those six fields are resolved may the lock include an aesthetic specification for subject, morphology, silhouette, dominant paths, layering, materials, color, focus, authorized signature, and verbatim prohibitions. Do not expose the internal specification unless the user requests it.

## 5. PRE-EXECUTION AND POST-EXECUTION GATES

### 5.1. Pre-execution gate

Execute only after the router has loaded every required source, conflicts are resolved, the operation lock is complete, critical negatives have constructive equivalents where needed, and the stop condition is explicit. If a required answer is missing, stop. Do not reconstruct it from this file or conversation memory.

### 5.2. Post-execution verification

After the tool returns an image, verify only from visible evidence. The generated image has no authority. No correction or regeneration may occur without rerunning the full CI route.

### Gate A — Hard compliance

- Are counts, construction, required colors, required objects, and prohibitions satisfied?
- Did the model add any unrequested genre-default detail?

Aesthetic polish never excuses a hard failure.

### Gate B — Large-form read

- Does silhouette and primary focus remain legible at small scale?
- Do garment layers build form, or are they merely adjacent items?

### Gate C — Body–clothing linkage

- Do body and garment lines extend, counterbalance, or intentionally interrupt one another?
- Does any region look pasted on or arbitrarily concealed?

### Gate D — Color and material

- Does the palette have hierarchy?
- Do materials behave distinctly and clarify layer order?

### Gate E — Coherence

- Do proportions, line, pose, color, material, and detail share one language?
- Does any attractive part appear to belong to another design?

### Gate F — Attraction under this configuration

Pass only when attraction comes from the locked relationship among silhouette, proportion, curve, layering, color, and material. If it depends mainly on exposed skin, anatomical exaggeration, or sexualized posing, fail it. Redesign only when the operation and allowed scope permit it; otherwise report the exact defect.

### Gate G — Detail and accessory economy

- Does every visible accessory cluster have an established function?
- Is any item only filling space, repeating an existing idea, or leaking from genre convention?
- Do accessories weaken the silhouette or create competing focal points?
- Is detail concentrated by region rather than spread uniformly?
- Are hair, garment body, footwear, and accessories all mechanically over-detailed?
- Would removing a secondary item actually weaken structure or identity?

Unnecessary accessories that do not alter structure are `LOCAL_FAIL`. A design built primarily through accessory accumulation instead of silhouette, layering, and material is `STRUCTURAL_FAIL`.

### 5.3. Result classification

- `PASS`: every relevant hard constraint visibly passes.
- `LOCAL_FAIL`: one bounded defect can be corrected without reopening the design; reopen only the failed field.
- `STRUCTURAL_FAIL`: identity, count, anatomy, topology, silhouette, composition, or another invariant failed; regenerate from the resolved operation lock while explicitly rejecting the failed interpretation.
- `UNVERIFIABLE`: visible evidence is insufficient to judge a hard constraint; do not claim compliance.

Every correction, retry, variation, or regeneration must rerun `ROUTE → LOAD → RESOLVE → LOCK → COMPOSE → EXECUTE → VERIFY`.

## 6. DEFAULT EXCLUSIONS

Unless explicitly requested, do not add:

- exposed cleavage, exposed thighs, or deep cutouts;
- full-body skin-tight clothing;
- implausibly narrow waist, extremely elongated legs, or enlarged breasts or hips;
- arched-back, chest-forward, hip-twisted, or inviting poses;
- oily skin lighting;
- excessive straps, buckles, jewelry, patterns, or fabric layers;
- several competing accent colors;
- generic luxury or high-fashion detailing without a structural role;
- contemporary realistic human anatomy imposed on cartoon or nonhuman morphology.

---

## 7. FINAL PASS CRITERIA

An image passes only when every hard constraint passes; the silhouette is strong and legible; body and clothing form one system; layering has function and hierarchy; color, value, and material reinforce structure; style is coherent without becoming anonymous; attraction exists at whole-form level even under high coverage; the result does not rely on sexualized shortcuts or common image-model tropes; and every non-required accessory has a defensible structural, semantic, focal, rhythmic, or material role.

If separate details pass but the whole does not form one coherent structure, the result fails.

---

## 8. KNOWN FAILURE MODES

When relevant, place these risks in forbidden transformations or verification:

- increasing exposed skin, tightness, or sexualized posing to replace weak structure;
- separating body and clothing into non-interacting layers;
- adding accessories, straps, patterns, or effects to simulate complexity;
- applying identical physical behavior to every material;
- replacing structural curves with anatomical exaggeration;
- leaking unassigned attributes from a reference;
- expanding a local correction into a redesign;
- accumulating several low-impact choices into a new design direction;
- obscuring a region to evade count, anatomy, topology, or attachment checks;
- treating beauty or familiarity as evidence of compliance;
- generating a complete accessory bundle from a style, period, culture, or occupation label;
- using straps, buckles, tassels, metal trim, jewelry, or insignia to fill quiet areas;
- placing accessories symmetrically merely because one side is empty;
- repeating one motif across hair, neck, waist, wrists, and footwear without adding a relationship;
- turning every junction into a belt, strap, or buckle;
- using all-over pattern to simulate identity;
- adding oversized headwear or jewelry to force a focal point;
- giving hair, clothing, footwear, and accessories uniformly high detail density;
- mixing styles by accumulating surface symbols;
- inventing a function, history, or meaning for an unauthorized detail.

This configuration completes its role when it has constrained every relevant aesthetic choice without independently inventing identity, structure, content, props, setting, or a design direction not established by an authorized source.

After the operation stop condition is met, do not use coherence, signature, attraction, or whole-form quality as permission to continue editing, beautifying, adding, or expanding the result.
