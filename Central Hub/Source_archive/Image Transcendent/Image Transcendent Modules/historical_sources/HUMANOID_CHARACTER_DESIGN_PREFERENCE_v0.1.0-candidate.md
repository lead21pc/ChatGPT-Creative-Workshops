## Selection, scope and authority

This candidate is not an active Project source or a replacement for config.md. Use its full text only when explicitly selected for the current experiment, or under a separately approved activation rule. No such routing rule is implemented in Phase 1. Presence, subject similarity and prior selection never activate it. Selection expires with the current operation.

Apply only to human or humanoid character design and only to components actually present. An unclothed subject does not acquire garments; an accessory-free subject does not acquire accessories. Other domains receive no preference influence.

These are soft aesthetic directions, subordinate to the current request, assigned reference roles, active template fields, global anti-prior, generic anti-drift and the operation lock. ## Relational primitives

Use large form before detail and relationships before isolated attractiveness. The following directions describe existing or independently authorized choices, not a checklist of new elements to add.

### Silhouette, proportions and curves — P01, P02, P03

Read the figure as a legible large mass with intentional rhythm, balance and direction. Relate head, shoulders, torso, limbs and existing clothing mass within the authorized proportional system. Treat curves as continuous visual paths; describe how garments extend or counterbalance them. Do not assign new measurements or enlarge anatomy to achieve the preference.

### Body, clothing, layering and coverage — P04, P05, P06

Let existing clothing receive, extend, counterbalance or intentionally interrupt body lines. Relate seams, edges and existing openings to the form beneath. Each authorized layer should build mass, depth, proportion or rhythm, with clear overlap and readable layer order. Coverage can support attraction through proportion, layering and material relationships; this does not authorize new exposure, openings, layers or tighter clothing.

### Line, rhythm and focus — P07, P08, P09

Relate contour, seams, hair and existing accessories to an authorized line family; do not select a missing line family. Use quiet regions and controlled detail density to support rhythm. Refine focal hierarchy only if composition is opened to change. The face need not dominate when the authorized focus is silhouette or clothing.

### Color, value, materials and drape — P10, P11, P12, P13

Relate existing colors by dominant, supporting and accent functions only where those roles exist; monochrome does not need an accent. Use authorized value relationships to clarify silhouette and layer order. Describe the drape, tension, thickness, reflectivity and fold behavior of established materials. Rigid and soft materials should behave distinctly within the authorized visual language. Folds should relate to suspension, tension, compression or motion, rather than decorative density. Do not choose an unresolved material or lighting setup to fulfill this direction.

### Detail and accessory discipline — P14, P15

Keep fine detail subordinate to the large silhouette. Describe each authorized accessory through its actual contribution to line, mass, rhythm, function or focus. Prefer regional detail hierarchy over uniform ornament. Suggest reducing optional detail only inside fields opened to change; never delete a required element. No accessory quota, new accessory cluster or invented function is supplied by this module.

### Pose linkage and style coherence — P16, P17

Relate the authorized pose to weight, body axis, silhouette and garment fall. Describe how established proportion, line, material, color and stylization work together. Intentional mixed styles remain valid; reconcile their authorized relationships only within allowed variables. Do not select a new pose or import a convention bundle to make the whole coherent.

### Authorized signature and whole-form linkage — P18, P20, P21

Preserve an established signature through relationships among authorized parts. New identity markers require separate authorization. Relate existing hair/headwear, neckline and shoulders; relate hem or trousers to existing footwear, feet and ground contact. These relationships do not require adding missing components or decorative symmetry. Evaluate the whole rather than the number of individually satisfied preferences; report a conflict outside allowed variables rather than redesigning it.
