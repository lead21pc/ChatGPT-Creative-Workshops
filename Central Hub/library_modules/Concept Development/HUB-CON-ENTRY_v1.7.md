Use the shortest complete path needed: premise/material change -> affected actor or system -> relevant constraints/information -> response or interaction -> consequence -> feedback/adaptation when material -> resulting state. Establish the links; do not choose an endpoint and rationalize backward. Hidden knowledge, motives or future events require an information path, not author omniscience. Mark unsupported links without inventing their resolution.

When the current task assigns examination or development within the same premise, establish its relevant state, protected assumptions, constraints and material unknowns. Check it against established definitions, timeline, relations, capabilities, limits, interfaces, dependencies and prior consequences. Trace the shortest sufficient causal path through affected actors and systems. Establish information, authority and capability before inferring a response that depends on them.

Use the consequences of the existing premise to identify what needs further development. Elaborate only the consequences, dependencies or missing mechanisms needed for the assigned task, then check how the elaboration affects the resulting state. Preserve unsupported links as unknowns, proposals, conditional branches or unresolved dependencies under the existing state rules. Introduce a primitive only when the required behavior cannot emerge from the current state; record its assumptions and costs as a proposal unless accepted. If progress requires changing a constitutive assumption or introducing an independent driver, identify that need and keep any proposed change conditional until accepted. Carry through supported consequences at whatever scale the mechanism produces. Stop once the assigned question has enough causal development. Do not add material for volume, novelty, spectacle, generic familiarity or genre escalation. Concept supplied as context alone does not assign development.

For other authorized expansion, establish the relevant state and unknowns, add the development the task needs and propagate its effects. Explore secondary effects where useful. Introduce a primitive only when the required behavior cannot emerge from the current state; keep it a proposal with relevant assumptions and costs unless accepted. For concept analysis/audit, examine only relevant assumption conflicts, definition drift, contradictions, false dependencies, authority/information gaps, causal gaps, circularity, feedback/failure and edge/interface conflict. Distinguish flaws, trade-offs, missing state and conditional results. Findings do not authorize redesign; keep the task's audit boundary.
