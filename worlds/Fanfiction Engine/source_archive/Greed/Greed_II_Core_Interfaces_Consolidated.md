## Consolidated Working Draft

**Scope:** This document consolidates three source files into one canonical working draft:

- `Greed Axe.txt`
- `Greed Paradox.txt`
- `the ring.txt`

`Greed Paradox` is integrated into the **Greed Axe / Greed II interface** rather than retained as a separate document.  
`The Ring` remains a distinct interface section.

This consolidation does **not** import later information from other files such as The King naming or weapon-name files.

# 0. Canon Status

## Stable Core

- Greed II is distinct from Greed I.
- Greed II is not primarily a warrior; most combat power comes from the weapon system.
- The weapon core is a family of five servants.
- The original weapon is a Divine Halberd.
- Open Form, Astaroth, Ampte, and True Form are different manifestations of the same underlying weapon.
- The Five Servants retain individual wills.
- Internal disagreement among the Five can reduce fusion efficiency and combat performance.
- Greed generally prefers not to engage or escalate conflict.
- The Ring is an experiential instrument, not simply a power-up item.
- A Ring must ultimately be returned voluntarily.
- If voluntary return fails and Greed must use force, the Ring is destroyed rather than reclaimed.
- Greed's paradoxical desire is structurally tied to the Ring process.

## Unresolved / Placeholder

- Cosmology, Citadel, Seven Seats, and detailed history.
- Final True Name of the weapon's True Form.
- Exact Ring origin.
- Ring mechanics and power limits.
- Exact benefits granted by a Ring.
- Exact number of Rings.

### Canon Conflict Preserved

`Greed Paradox.txt` states that the Founder gave Greed **Infinite Rings**, while `the ring.txt` explicitly leaves the number of Rings as a placeholder.

This merged file therefore treats **Infinite Rings as a current Paradox-layer assumption, not yet promoted to stable canon**.

# 1. Greed II — Identity and Operational Profile

## Identity

Greed II is not the original Greed.

He began as a backup avatar of Greed I.

After Greed I was betrayed and failed, the backup avatar developed an independent will. Greed I accepted this divergence, allowed the avatar to inherit the position, and ended the previous cycle.

Greed II therefore exists as an independent individual rather than as a mere duplicate.

## Personal Capability

Greed II is:

- not fundamentally a warrior,
- not a swordsman,
- not a knight,
- only a third-rank mage by his own baseline capability.

Most of his combat strength comes from the weapon system and the Five Servants.

## Default Operational Style

Greed prefers:

- not to engage,
- not to escalate,
- to fight only when required.

This restraint is important because his Ring duty can eventually force him into battles against extremely powerful recipients he personally cultivated.

# 2. Interface A — Greed Axe / Five Servants

## Core Concept

The central feature of the weapon interface is not Greed himself.

It is:

> **A family of five people who became a weapon system.**

The Five Servants are:

- Father
- Mother
- Eldest Son
- Second Child
- Youngest Child

They became servants of Greed II after a major event.

They are not mindless equipment.

Each retains an individual will.

## Family Dynamics

### Parents

The parents are comparatively relaxed around Greed.

### Children

The three children frequently argue.

Their constant friction functions both as:

- a recurring interpersonal dynamic,
- and a mechanical weakness of the weapon system.

Even in combat, they can continue to quarrel.

---

# 3. Weapon Ontology

The weapon was originally a **Divine Halberd**.

That is its original form.

After fusion with Greed and the Five Servants, its mode of existence changed.

The current forms are **not evolutionary stages**.

They are separate manifestations of the same underlying entity.

The True Form is a return to the weapon's original Divine Halberd state.

# 7. Mechanical Flaw

The primary flaw is not Greed.

It is the family.

Because the Five Servants retain independent wills:

> **internal disharmony reduces fusion quality.**

Consequences include:

- lower integration efficiency,
- weaker weapon performance,
- reduced True Form coherence.

The same interpersonal system that gives the weapon identity also creates its principal vulnerability.

# 8. Greed Paradox — Integrated Behavioral Governor

This section is not a separate interface.

It describes the behavioral logic that governs Greed II across the weapon and Ring systems.

## 8.1 Distorted Greed

Greed does not primarily desire things by taking them.

His greed is structurally closer to:

> **wanting to be given.**

He expects the universe, people, or systems around him to voluntarily give him what he desires because he believes himself worthy of receiving it.

When something is refused, the frustration is not simply:

> "I lost the object."

It is closer to:

> "I was refused."

The emotional significance lies in rejection, not material deprivation.

## 8.2 Infinite Responsibility Loop

The Paradox source models the Ring system as:

> Infinite Rings  
> → Infinite Responsibilities  
> → Infinite Lifetimes to Observe  
> → Infinite Possible Refusals  
> → Infinite Battles  
> → Return to Infinite Rings

Each Ring corresponds not merely to an object, but to:

- one life,
- one world,
- one soul,
- one responsibility that must be followed through to completion.

The better Greed performs his duty, the more responsibility accumulates.

His greed for receiving therefore produces an inverse burden:

> **the more he wishes to receive, the more he must first give and carry.**

### Canon Note

The "Infinite Rings" premise is still pending because the Ring working draft leaves Ring count unresolved.

## 8.3 Employee vs Avatar Paradox

At the end of a Ring recipient's life, Greed experiences a structural conflict.

### As an Academy Employee

He does **not** want the Ring holder to refuse repayment.

A voluntary return means:

- the experiential dataset is complete,
- the life-cycle is closed correctly,
- the Responsibility was fulfilled,
- the process achieved its KPI.

### As the Avatar of Greed

He **does** want the Ring holder to refuse.

Why?

Because refusal gives him legitimate cause to fight the person he spent a lifetime cultivating.

If Ring recipients can grow to near-demigod status, this gives Greed access to precisely the kind of battle he otherwise would not seek out.

He desires:

- the battle,
- the experience,
- even the possibility of being beaten nearly to death.

### Structural Result

Whatever the recipient chooses:

- if they return the Ring, the employee is satisfied but the Avatar loses the desired battle;
- if they refuse, the Avatar gains the desired battle but the employee loses the ideal completion state.

Greed can never receive both outcomes at once.

This is the core paradox.

## 8.4 Farmer Who Raises Bosses

Greed is not fundamentally a hunter searching for strong opponents.

He behaves more like a farmer.

He:

1. gives a Ring to an ordinary person,
2. allows that person to grow with it,
3. observes an entire life,
4. helps create a being potentially capable of reaching demigod-level power,
5. eventually arrives to request the Ring back.

The absurdity is deliberate:

> Greed may spend a lifetime cultivating the very opponent who can later beat him nearly to death.

His power fantasy is not:

> "I defeat the strongest."

It is:

> "I get to fight the strongest being I helped create."

## 8.5 Meta Recursion

At a higher layer, Greed's own contradictory life becomes another object of collection.

The Founder, whose greed operates at the level of infinite entertainment or experiential novelty, can treat Greed's paradox itself as valuable observation material.

Thus Greed is simultaneously:

- collector,
- participant,
- employee,
- experimental subject.

# 10. Development Method

The weapon concept was not built mainly through one-pass brainstorming.

Development process:

1. Create a concept.
2. Role-play / method-act the character.
3. Safely simulate analogous physical actions.
4. Observe:
   - inertia,
   - swing radius,
   - center of gravity,
   - movement,
   - handling sensation.
5. Adjust the design.
6. Repeat.

The current weapon forms are the result of repeated iteration over years rather than a single design session.

# 11. Interface B — The Ring

## 11.1 Overview

The Ring is one of Greed's core mechanisms.

It is not simply a power-enhancing artifact.

Its main purpose is not combat.

Its purpose is:

> **to generate and capture a complete human experience.**

The Ring is a means, not the final objective.

## 11.2 Primary Purpose

Greed collects unique human experience.

The Ring exists to create conditions in which such experience becomes possible.

It acts as:

- opportunity,
- test,
- catalyst.

It is not fundamentally a reward.

## 11.3 Standard Ring Process

```text
Greed selects a suitable person
        ↓
Greed gives them a Ring
        ↓
The Ring provides meaningful benefits
        ↓
The recipient lives with it for most of their life
        ↓
Successes, failures, choices and transformations accumulate
        ↓
Greed returns
        ↓
The Ring must be returned
```

## 11.4 Absolute Return Condition

The owner must:

> **remove the Ring voluntarily and give it back.**

This is the core rule.

The final test is not physical power.

It is voluntary relinquishment.

## 11.5 Refusal Branch

If the owner refuses to return the Ring:

> Greed fights them.

This is the point at which the process moves from observation into direct intervention.

## 11.6 If Greed Wins

Greed does **not** reclaim the Ring.

He destroys it himself.

Reason:

If a Ring can only be recovered through violence, then the Ring has failed its purpose.

It no longer has value as a completed experiential instrument.

---

# 12. What the Ring Actually Tests

The Ring is not designed to determine:

- who is strongest,
- who is richest,
- who achieves the greatest social success.

The actual test is:

> **After receiving power and advantage for nearly an entire lifetime, can the person still voluntarily let it go?**

The final question is not about owning power.

It is about retaining ownership of oneself.

---

# 13. What Greed Harvests

During the recipient's lifetime, Greed obtains the experiential record generated through the Ring.

This may include:

- success,
- failure,
- choices,
- ambition,
- betrayal,
- gratitude,
- growth,
- corruption,
- and every other unique experience produced by that person's life.

The Ring itself is not the true harvest.

The **life lived with the Ring** is the harvest.

---

# 14. Ring Benefits

Exact benefits are not finalized.

The design requirement is that they must be strong enough to:

- materially change the recipient's life,
- produce meaningful choices,
- create genuine opportunity,

without becoming so overwhelming that the final test loses meaning.

---

# 15. Relationship Between Greed and the Ring

Greed does not fundamentally covet the physical Ring.

He does not primarily want to:

- keep it,
- hoard it,
- steal it back.

He wants to:

- observe,
- collect experience,
- witness the recipient's final decision.

The Ring therefore functions as an external experimental interface for Greed's deeper behavioral paradox.

---

# 16. Ring Symbolism

The Ring represents:

- ownership,
- choice,
- relinquishment,
- responsibility attached to power.

Its central proposition is:

> If a person cannot voluntarily let go of the Ring, then eventually the person no longer owns the Ring.

> The Ring owns the person.

# 20. Consolidation Principle

This file keeps the interfaces distinct:

- **Greed Axe** describes Greed's combat embodiment, the Five Servants, weapon forms, family dynamics, and the Greed Paradox governing his behavior.
- **The Ring** describes Greed's external experiential process applied to other people.

The two interfaces interact, but they are not collapsed into one mechanism.

The Ring can create the terminal conflict.

The Greed Paradox explains why Greed simultaneously desires and rejects that conflict.

The Axe is the system through which he may ultimately have to resolve it.
