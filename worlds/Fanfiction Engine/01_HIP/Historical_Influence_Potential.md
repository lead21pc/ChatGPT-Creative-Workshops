# Historical Influence Potential (HIP)

## Definition

**Historical Influence Potential (HIP)** is an open evaluation gateway used to determine the historical influence potential of an individual, entity, anomaly, or other target through externally supplied criteria.

HIP itself does **not** define:

- what counts as *potential*;
- what counts as *influence*;
- what degree of influence is considered significant;
- what form historical change must take;
- what criteria qualify or disqualify a target.

Those definitions are intentionally delegated to the specific evaluation framework, world, context, or criterion set in which HIP is applied.

## Core Principle

HIP is **criteria-agnostic**.

It does not prescribe a universal metric of historical importance. Instead, it provides a gateway through which different criterion sets can evaluate the same abstract question:

> Does this target possess Historical Influence Potential under the criteria currently being applied?

## Engine Role

HIP is designed as an engine component rather than a fixed scoring system. Its purpose is to remain open enough to be mapped onto different realities, institutions, civilizations, anomalies, or operational contexts without forcing them to share one universal definition of historical influence.

This allows different applications to define their own evaluation logic while preserving HIP as the common interface.

## Important Distinction

HIP does not claim that a target **will** alter history, nor does it define how such alteration must occur. It only provides the abstract evaluation layer through which a context-specific system may determine whether a target qualifies as historically influential or potentially influential.

A Regressor, for example, may be classified as having very high HIP under one criterion set, but that classification does not redefine HIP itself or imply that all high-HIP targets must resemble Regressors.

## Short Definition

> **Historical Influence Potential (HIP): An open evaluation gateway for determining historical influence potential through externally supplied criteria. HIP itself does not prescribe what constitutes potential, influence, significance, or qualification.**
