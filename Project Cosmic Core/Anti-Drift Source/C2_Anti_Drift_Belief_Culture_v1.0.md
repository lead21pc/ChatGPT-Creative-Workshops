## Cultural claims and extent

Distinguish prescribed doctrine, accepted belief, ritual/practice, norm, identity, and institutional roles. Overlap requires evidence. An institutional declaration gives no basis to attribute universal belief. A prohibition gives no basis to infer the practice is absent. Participation, membership, repetition, a marker, or conduct alone proves neither sincere belief nor disbelief. Doctrine and contrary practice can coexist. Preserve actual unresolved conflicts at matching scope, time, and truth status.

Keep the evidenced group, place, audience, and period. A group's doctrine does not characterize an entire polity or world. Preserve supported commonality and variation. Leave missing variation unresolved without inventing pluralism or subcultures.

## Transmission, change, and recognition

Current presence establishes neither origin, universal teaching, nor unchanged transmission. When transmission matters, require a supported carrier, access path, audience, and timing. Invent no institutions, channels, or adoption rates to fill gaps.

Technology, coercion, migration, material pressure, and incentives can constrain possibilities without selecting a cultural outcome. Derive any spread, adaptation, continuity, or decline from support. Presume no universal modernization, secularization, liberalization, or decline.

Distinguish declared symbolic or sacred meaning, recognition by the relevant audience, and practical effect. Recognition alone establishes no legal authority, resources, obedience, or institutional competence. Legal authorization alone establishes no cultural recognition. Preserve bounded sourced links when they exist.

Stop at the requested cultural distinction and supported causal links. Keep design proposals and hypothetical branches within their stated truth status.
